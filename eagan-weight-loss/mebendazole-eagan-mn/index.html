    How Mebendazole Treatment Works in Eagan, MN

    How Mebendazole Works in Eagan

    Mebendazole blocks a worm's ability to take in glucose by disrupting tiny structures inside its cells called microtubules. The worms starve and die. They pass in the stool over the following days, and most people never notice them, which is normal and does not mean the drug failed. Very little of the drug reaches the bloodstream. In the US, it is sold as an FDA-approved chewable tablet.

    Who Mebendazole Is For

    Adults usually ask for it because of pinworm. Nighttime anal itching, especially when kids at home have the same complaint, is the classic sign. It also treats roundworm, whipworm and hookworm, which are more often linked to travel or certain environments. Adults with a positive test qualify. It is not a detox and has no role in weight loss.

    Mebendazole Dosing and Schedule in Eagan

    For pinworm, the usual plan is a single 100 mg chewable tablet, repeated in two weeks. That second dose catches worms that hatched from eggs the first dose could not reach. Other intestinal worms are often treated with 100 mg twice daily for three days, followed by a stool test in some cases. Chew it fully. Meals do not matter.

    What to Expect After Taking Mebendazole

    Most people feel nothing after the dose. Some get mild stomach discomfort. Itching usually eases within a few days, and it can take another week for the skin to calm down completely, especially if it was scratched a lot. You may not see worms. Put the two week dose in your phone calendar the day you take the first.

    Side Effects of Mebendazole in Eagan

    Because so little is absorbed, mebendazole is well tolerated at short courses. Possible side effects include abdominal pain, diarrhea, gas and nausea. Rash is rare. Longer, high-dose courses used for other conditions carry added risks, like lower white blood cell counts or liver effects, which is why providers stick to standard dosing here. Report fever or a spreading rash the same day.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake asks about symptoms, timing, household members, recent travel and any test results. Pregnancy is a key question. Mebendazole is generally avoided in the first trimester, and the provider also screens for breastfeeding, liver disease and interacting drugs. Metronidazole is the big interaction, since combining the two has been linked to rare severe skin reactions. Non-pinworm infections usually need a stool test first.

    How Mebendazole Compares to Other Options in Eagan

    Albendazole works the same way and is sometimes used for the same infections. Over-the-counter pyrantel pamoate is another pinworm option that paralyzes the worms rather than starving them. Ivermectin is preferred for strongyloides, scabies and lice, rather than pinworm. When the type of parasite is still unclear, the antiparasitic protocol starts with that question and may recommend testing. Your provider recommends the drug with the best evidence.
