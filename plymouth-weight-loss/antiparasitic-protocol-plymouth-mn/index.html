    How the Antiparasitic Protocol Works in Plymouth

    The protocol starts with your symptoms and possible exposures, not with a pill. The provider asks which parasites make sense given where you have traveled, what pets you have and whether people in your household have the same complaints. Testing comes next when it would help. A stool ova and parasite test or a targeted antigen test may be recommended, and the provider explains how to arrange one. Treatment uses prescription anti-parasitics with established uses, and a follow-up confirms symptoms are gone.

    Who This Protocol Is For

    Adults with symptoms or exposures that raise a fair concern for a parasite are the right fit. Nighttime anal itching in a household with kids, which usually means pinworm, is the most common reason people start. Recent travel to regions where intestinal worms are common is another, as is a positive stool test from a previous clinic. It is not a detox. It has no role in weight loss either.

    Dosing and Schedule in Plymouth

    The schedule depends on the drug and the parasite. Pinworm is commonly treated with one dose of mebendazole 100 mg or albendazole 400 mg, repeated after two weeks to catch worms that hatched after the first dose. Ivermectin depends on your body weight. Most ivermectin plans are a single dose or two days in a row. Your provider writes out exact dates for every dose.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Most courses take one to three days, sometimes with a repeat dose two weeks later. A day or two of mild stomach upset or tiredness after dosing is common. With pinworm, the itching usually eases within a few days, and the remaining irritation can take another week to settle. Week two is the repeat dose. A check or a follow-up test may be suggested about a month after treatment.

    Side Effects and Precautions in Plymouth

    Nausea, abdominal pain, diarrhea and headache are the side effects people mention most often. Ivermectin can occasionally cause rash or itching, especially as parasites die off. Because mebendazole and albendazole stay mostly in the gut at standard doses, short courses rarely cause much trouble. Pregnancy changes things. Some anti-parasitics are avoided in pregnancy, so tell your provider if there is any chance.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake asks when symptoms started, where you have traveled in the past year and whether pets or family members are involved. Upload any previous stool test results. Providers also screen for pregnancy, breastfeeding, liver disease and current medications that could interact, such as warfarin with ivermectin. Severe abdominal pain, blood in the stool or a high fever mean in-person care. Some people are asked to finish testing before any medication is prescribed.

    How the Protocol Compares to Over-the-Counter Cleanses in Plymouth

Prescription drugs come with set dosing. St. Paul Weight Loss also offers ivermectin and mebendazole on their own, and each one is matched to a different set of infections. Your provider picks one drug rather than layering several products.
